Bézier Spline Studio

Splitting a Bézier at a parameter t produces two Bézier pieces — the left and right edges of the de Casteljau triangle. Here the split is committed: the two pieces become independently editable control polygons joined at a shared point (C0). Drag the shared point to move both pieces; toggle C1 to couple the two tangent handles so the joint stays smooth. After the split, t no longer changes the shape — it only slides the two evaluation points CL(t) and CR(t) along the fixed pieces.

C0 continuity (always on) means the two pieces literally share the join point — move it and both curves move. C1 continuity (the toggle) adds tangent continuity: the two control points either side of the join are mirror images across it, so dragging one snaps the other and the joint has no crease. With C1 off every point moves freely, so you can see the difference between a merely joined (C0) and a smooth (C1) spline. Subdivision itself is the de Casteljau triangle: the left piece's net is the triangle's left edge, the right piece's net its right edge.
