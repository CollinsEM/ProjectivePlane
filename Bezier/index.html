Bézier Curve Studio

A Bézier curve is built from its control net by the de Casteljau algorithm: at a parameter t, each level replaces a polygon by the polygon of segment points (1−t)·A + t·B. The polygons shrink toward a single point — C(t). Add and drag points and watch the nested linear approximations converge.

With n+1 control points you get a Bézier curve of degree n. The de Casteljau construction has n levels: the control net (level 0) is replaced n times by shorter polygons until one point remains — that point is C(t) on the curve. Every C(t) lies inside the convex hull of the net. Moving any control point reshapes the whole curve — a Bézier has no local control. Elevate re-expresses the same curve at a higher degree — repeated elevation pulls the net onto the curve. Splitting a curve into two editable pieces (subdivision) with C0 / C1 continuity lives in the companion BezierSpline demo.
